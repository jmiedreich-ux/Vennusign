# Restaurant Baseline

Restaurant is the canonical baseline inherited by every later native-industry profile.

## Business types

Quick service, fast casual, full service, fine dining, buffet, and food hall or multi-concept.

## Primary outcomes

- Keep content accurate.
- Change prices and availability quickly.
- Publish reliably to selected screens.
- Promote specials and high-margin items.
- Adjust presentation by service period.
- Confirm every screen shows the intended content.

## Core capabilities

- Menu, category, and item creation, editing, duplication, archive, and restore.
- Prices, descriptions, images, and dietary labels.
- Manual availability and mobile or desktop Quick Update.
- Basic specials.
- Screen pairing and management.
- Explicit screen targeting.
- Preview and immediate publish.
- Delivery confirmation and current status.
- Offline and outdated detection.
- Undo or restoration of the prior published version.
- Basic layouts and themes.
- Business hours and venue information.
- Understandable errors and recovery guidance.

## Candidate tier capabilities

Scheduling, dayparts, recurring specials, advanced themes and layouts, campaigns, coordinated screens, video walls, multi-venue sharing, brand controls, reusable libraries, approvals, advanced permissions, audit history, analytics, and organization dashboards.

## Candidate add-ons

POS synchronization, AI-assisted content or design, managed hardware coverage, premium analytics or data, custom integrations, and enterprise identity where independently justified.

## Limits

Venue count, screen count, user count, storage, retained history, AI usage, and integration connections.

## Permissions

Who may change prices or availability, publish, manage screens, approve changes, manage users, purchase plans or add-ons, and change billing.

## Product states

Availability, draft or published state, online or offline state, pairing state, delivery state, special state, and campaign state.

## Inheritance rule

A later industry inherits all of this unless its industry document explicitly records a meaningful difference, removal, qualification, renamed concept, or additional requirement.
