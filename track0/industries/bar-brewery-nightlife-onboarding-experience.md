# Bar, Brewery & Nightlife Onboarding Experience

## Authority and scope

This document defines the non-implementation onboarding contract for Bar, Brewery & Nightlife under RWP-00.23. It uses the merged industry, subtype, terminology, operating, capability-classification, and proposed packaging records. It does not reactivate RWP-13.06 or authorize UI, API, schema, billing, entitlement, or product implementation.

Industry and subtype personalize recommendations. They never grant commercial access. Essential setup and a useful first-screen path remain available without buying optional capabilities or connecting an external system.

## Intended onboarding outcome

A new operator should finish with:

- the correct organization and venue selected or created;
- Bar, Brewery & Nightlife selected as the local business profile when appropriate;
- one primary subtype or a neutral fallback, plus optional descriptive traits for hybrids;
- venue identity, local time, service model, and the smallest useful operating schedule;
- recognizable starter content for menus, taps, specials, events, entry guidance, or venue information;
- at least one paired screen or a deliberate pairing deferral with an exact next action;
- explicit target selection, preview, publication, and target-level delivery confirmation;
- clear access to Quick Update, correction, retry, and restoration;
- contextual optional-plan and add-on discovery only after the included core path is understood.

## Experience principles

1. Ask only what is needed to make the first screen useful.
2. Use operator language such as venue, tap list, happy hour, doors, event, and screen—not internal entitlement language.
3. Allow unknown, not-applicable, and decide-later answers.
4. Preserve entered content through back, resume, retry, subtype change, and interrupted sessions.
5. Do not force a POS, inventory, tap, reservation, ticketing, identity, sports-data, AI, hardware, or managed-service connection.
6. Do not force pricing or a final tier decision before the first useful content-and-screen path.
7. Never infer targets from subtype, tier, organization membership, or physical proximity.
8. Never claim successful publication without per-target delivery evidence.
9. Keep urgent manual operation available regardless of optional setup state.
10. Design for late-night, low-light, noisy, crowded, mobile, and intermittent-connectivity use.

## Recommended sequence

### 1. Confirm organization, venue, and authority

Show the organization and local venue being configured. Support authorized creation, selection of an existing venue, and recovery from invitation or identity problems. Explain view, edit, controlled-wording, screen-management, publish, restore, and administrative restrictions without presenting permission as a purchase problem.

For mixed organizations, the venue selects its own business profile while the organization retains the approved commercial subscription policy. A venue profile change must not change the subscription, delete content, reassign screens, or grant authority.

### 2. Select the local industry profile

Present **Bar, Brewery & Nightlife** when beverage-led service, late operating periods, events, entry context, or nightlife controls the guest experience. Briefly explain the practical defaults it supplies:

- beverage and optional food content;
- rapid availability and sold-out updates;
- cross-midnight hours and service periods;
- specials, releases, happy hours, events, and entertainment;
- venue areas and screen purposes;
- entry, reservation-information, and locally approved responsible wording;
- publishing, delivery confidence, offline awareness, and recovery.

Industry selection changes defaults and terminology only.

### 3. Select a primary subtype and hybrid traits

Offer the approved primary subtypes and a neutral fallback:

- Bar / Pub;
- Brewery / Brewpub;
- Taproom;
- Cocktail Bar;
- Wine Bar;
- Sports Bar;
- Nightclub;
- Lounge;
- Music / Entertainment Bar;
- Unspecified / General Bar, Brewery or Nightlife Venue.

Use short operational examples. Ask which model best describes day-to-day service and guest communication, not the legal license or marketing label.

Allow one primary subtype plus traits such as food-led, production-led, tasting-led, sports-led, live-entertainment-led, late-night, reservation-led, private-event, multi-room, seasonal, or hybrid. Traits tune recommendations; they do not stack tier entitlements or limits.

### 4. Name the venue and define service context

Collect the customer-facing venue name and, when useful, area names such as main bar, taproom, patio, lounge, stage, private room, viewing zone, tasting area, or kitchen.

Ask the smallest applicable service-model question:

- bar, counter, table, hybrid, or event-led service;
- food available or beverage-only;
- general reservation information shown or not shown;
- general cover, ticket, guest-list, or entry guidance needed or not needed;
- public responsible or access wording supplied by the venue.

Do not collect personal reservation, ticket, guest-list, payment, identity, or eligibility data during core onboarding.

### 5. Establish local time and operating periods

Confirm the venue time zone and operating-day boundary. Ask only for the first useful schedule:

- venue opening and closing;
- bar and kitchen periods when different;
- doors, event start, last entry, and venue-authored last-call information when used;
- cross-midnight behavior;
- one-off closure or special-hours needs.

Offer “set later” where safe. Do not invent jurisdictional last-call, age, access, or responsible-service policy.

### 6. Create starter content

Offer reversible starter paths:

- subtype-aware starter content;
- start blank;
- copy from another authorized venue;
- import from an already supported source;
- create a simple opening, hours, event, or coming-soon screen.

Starter content may include visibly marked samples for:

- drink categories and items;
- taps, pours, flights, bottle/can formats, cocktails, wine, and optional food;
- available, unavailable, and sold-out examples;
- a happy hour, release, tasting, game-day offer, or event;
- venue hours, doors, entry guidance, and a responsible public-information example;
- area or screen-purpose recommendations.

Sample content must never publish automatically. Operators can rename, remove, reorder, or replace every item.

### 7. Review required rapid controls

Use a short optional checklist rather than a long tour. Demonstrate:

- Quick Update for availability and sold-out state;
- current hours and one-off changes;
- special, event, delay, cancellation, relocation, pause, and resumption updates;
- target selection and contextual preview;
- publish confirmation and per-screen result;
- retry, correction, supersession, undo, and restore.

These are included core operations and must not be presented as premium features.

### 8. Select starter screen purposes and layouts

Recommend screen purposes from venue context without creating or targeting them automatically:

- drink menu or tap list;
- cocktail or wine list;
- food menu;
- specials and happy hour;
- events, entertainment, sports, or schedule;
- welcome and venue information;
- entry, cover, reservation-information, or wayfinding guidance;
- private-event or area-specific information.

Recommend accessible, distance-readable starter layouts with restrained motion and approved Sky Blue administrative styling. Public player presentation may use venue branding but must preserve contrast, legibility, safe areas, and fallback content.

### 9. Pair a screen or defer deliberately

Provide three explicit paths:

- pair a screen now;
- select an already authorized screen;
- continue with saved content and pair later.

Pairing must show device identity, venue, area or screen purpose, expected placement, and connectivity. Cover expired or incorrect code, already paired, permission denied, offline, unsupported, and retry states.

Deferral must preserve content and return the operator to a dashboard with one clear next action.

### 10. Select targets, preview, publish, and confirm

Require explicit target selection. Preview must show:

- venue, area, purpose, and screens;
- selected content and effective period;
- local time and cross-midnight impact;
- incomplete sample content or missing public wording;
- language and accessibility coverage;
- offline, outdated, unknown, permission, limit, or connection state;
- the exact publish action.

After publication, show accepted, pending, delivered, offline, outdated, failed, unknown, and partial states per target. Acknowledge the last successful version/time where known and provide retry, correct, restore, or continue-without-screen actions.

### 11. Introduce plan outcomes and add-ons contextually

Only after the operator has a useful content and screen path should onboarding introduce optional outcomes:

- plan recurring happy hours, releases, events, and campaigns;
- coordinate richer presentation and synchronized screens;
- scale libraries, approvals, brand governance, and analytics across venues;
- connect POS, inventory, taps, reservations, ticketing, guest lists, identity/access, sports/event data, AI, or external analytics;
- obtain managed hardware, connectivity, monitoring, installation, replacement, or enhanced support.

Explain what remains included. Distinguish upgrade required, add-on required, permission denied, limit reached, not configured, disconnected, stale, unsupported, and rollout-disabled. Purchase must not be required to complete core onboarding.

## Minimum onboarding questions

The minimum useful question set is:

1. Which organization and venue are being configured?
2. Is Bar, Brewery & Nightlife the correct local profile?
3. Which primary subtype best matches daily operation, or should it remain unspecified?
4. What customer-facing venue name should appear?
5. What is the venue time zone and smallest useful operating period?
6. Which content or simple starter screen should be created first?
7. Which screen purpose is intended?
8. Pair/select a screen now or defer?
9. Which exact target should receive the previewed content?

Food service, detailed areas, full schedules, every event type, integrations, advanced roles, pricing, analytics, and managed services are normally deferred.

## Subtype-aware defaults

- **Bar / Pub:** broad drinks, optional food, specials, events, hours, and general entry information.
- **Brewery / Brewpub:** house products, releases, flights, production/taproom context, optional food, tours or tastings.
- **Taproom:** taps, pours, flights, rapid availability, releases, and tasting/event information.
- **Cocktail Bar:** cocktail sections, ingredients/descriptions, specials, reservations information, lounge/table service.
- **Wine Bar:** glass/bottle formats, regions/styles, flights, tastings, food pairings, reservations information.
- **Sports Bar:** viewing zones, fixtures/events, game-day offers, delays, schedule changes, and area targeting.
- **Nightclub:** doors, events, lineup, cover/entry guidance, areas, private events, late-night periods.
- **Lounge:** table/lounge service, reservations information, specials, entertainment, private areas.
- **Music / Entertainment Bar:** lineup, doors, event times, pauses/cancellations, areas/stage, entry guidance.
- **Unspecified:** neutral menu, hours, event, venue-information, and screen-purpose defaults.

## Deferred questions and discoverable homes

Defer until after first-screen activation unless immediately required:

- full weekly and exception schedules;
- recurring event series and campaign calendars;
- all venue areas and screens;
- organization inheritance and brand governance;
- advanced roles, approvals, audit, and reporting;
- integrations, credentials, sources, mapping, and freshness rules;
- analytics retention and export;
- AI controls and consumption;
- managed hardware and support contracts;
- final tier, trial, add-on, or limit decisions.

Each deferred item must have a clear later location and must not block core operation.

## Trial-first dependencies without reactivating RWP-13.06

This RWP records only planning requirements:

- a valid trial may expose an approved set of capabilities without changing industry or subtype;
- trial expiration must preserve customer-authored data, current screen safety, manual core access, export/review, and a clear downgrade path;
- plan comparison must occur contextually after value is visible;
- no trial length, price, billing event, entitlement key, checkout flow, or post-trial implementation is approved here;
- RWP-13.06 remains paused until Track 0 consolidation and owner approval.

## Resume, failure, and recovery

Save progress after meaningful steps. Resume shows completed steps, unresolved required items, deferred items, draft content, pairing state, intended targets, last publish attempt/result, and safest next action.

Handle network interruption, expired session, changed permission, duplicate venue, invalid sample content, import failure, stale/conflicting source, pairing failure, failed/partial publication, limit restriction, unsupported device/provider/region, and user exit without discarding valid work. Provide retry, edit, skip when safe, save and exit, restore, or contact-admin actions.

## Accessibility and environment

Support phone, tablet, and desktop widths; keyboard-only operation; visible focus; screen-reader labels; logical headings; 200% zoom and reflow; long names; localization expansion; right-to-left readiness; non-color-only status; high contrast; low-light and glare conditions; reduced motion; explicit error summaries; and no unrecoverable timed step.

The Impeccable `shape`, `clarify`, and `harden` guidance applies: one dominant task per step, clear hierarchy and language, realistic content, complete states, safe confirmation, and actionable recovery.

## Completion definition

Onboarding is complete when the operator has either:

1. published verified content to at least one explicitly selected screen; or
2. created a valid venue and content draft, deliberately deferred pairing/publication, and received an exact next action.

Pricing, integrations, complete organization setup, every venue area, advanced analytics, and optional services are not completion prerequisites.

## Boundaries and handoff

Documentation and planning only. No UI, API, schema, migration, billing, entitlement, feature gate, trial, integration, analytics, player, or hardware implementation. RWP-13.06 remains paused.
