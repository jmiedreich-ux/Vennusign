# Food Truck & Concession Optional Capabilities

## Purpose

This document identifies capabilities that may improve scale, automation, coordination, insight, support, or commercial reach for Food Truck & Concession customers but are not required for ordinary manual operation.

Optional capabilities must not remove or degrade the required core set defined by RWP-00.43. Each concern is separated into a likely tier entitlement, independent add-on, quantity limit, product state, permission, or internal rollout control. Final packaging remains an owner decision.

## Optional capability principles

1. Optional capabilities should be introduced by customer outcome, not technical implementation.
2. A premium capability may accelerate or automate a task, but the corresponding essential manual task must remain available.
3. Integrations are independently purchasable or administratively enabled candidates unless a later owner decision explicitly bundles them.
4. Quantity and retention allowances are limits, not features.
5. Industry and subtype select recommendations only and never create hidden commercial access.

## Route, stop, and event scheduling

Optional scheduling candidates include:

- recurring routes and stop templates;
- planned stop sequences and reusable market or event calendars;
- multi-day festival, residency, and pop-up schedules;
- travel, setup, service, last-order, close, and teardown windows;
- conflict detection across units, staff, screens, hosts, and service points;
- bulk schedule publishing and exception handling;
- schedule approval and change history;
- reminders for setup, content review, location changes, and screen checks.

A basic manual current location, service period, and closure update remains core. Advanced scheduling is a tier candidate. External route, event, calendar, or venue synchronization is an add-on candidate.

## Location publishing and discovery

Optional location-publishing candidates include:

- public current-location and next-stop pages;
- embeddable location widgets;
- customer-facing route calendars;
- approved map links and directions;
- location subscription or notification workflows;
- multi-unit location directories;
- temporary or event-specific landing pages;
- location freshness, expiry, and approval controls.

These features must not imply live tracking unless a verified source supports it. Public publishing is a tier candidate; map, messaging, notification, or directory providers are add-on candidates. Number of public locations, pages, subscribers, or notifications may be limits.

## Advanced promotions and campaign automation

Optional promotion candidates include:

- scheduled daypart, event, weather, location, host, and inventory-aware campaigns;
- reusable promotion templates across units or events;
- sponsor and host-required content packages;
- promotion approval, expiration, and rollback;
- audience or venue-segment targeting;
- promotion performance comparison;
- external campaign ingestion;
- dynamic offer rotation where authoritative inputs exist.

Manual promotions and immediate content changes remain core. Advanced campaign orchestration is a tier candidate. Advertising, loyalty, coupon, sponsor, or campaign-platform integrations are add-on candidates.

## Multi-unit and multi-stand coordination

Optional coordination candidates include:

- organization-wide templates with local overrides;
- safe bulk menu, price, promotion, or notice changes;
- mixed-state visibility across units, stands, windows, and screens;
- exception-first monitoring;
- regional, event, host, or operator groupings;
- approvals and delegated publishing;
- coordinated launch, pause, rollback, and restoration;
- inheritance policies for brand, menu, price, sponsor, and host content;
- cross-unit schedule and resource views.

Single-unit operation remains core. Multi-unit orchestration and advanced approvals are tier candidates. Numbers of units, stands, groups, approvers, or bulk targets may be limits.

## Venue, event, and host integrations

Independent add-on candidates include:

- host event calendars and event metadata;
- stadium, arena, festival, convention, attraction, campus, airport, hotel, or resort location directories;
- gate, section, concourse, hall, zone, pitch, or stand assignments;
- sponsor and mandatory host-content feeds;
- venue opening, delay, cancellation, evacuation, or closure notices where authorized;
- event phases such as gates, intermission, halftime, sessions, or post-event service;
- host identity, approval, and source-authority workflows.

The integration must expose source, freshness, authority, failures, and manual override. Host data must not silently overwrite operator content.

## POS, ordering, payment, inventory, and production systems

Independent add-on candidates include:

- POS menu and price synchronization;
- sales-driven availability suggestions;
- inventory or ingredient-driven sell-out automation;
- digital ordering and payment links;
- order-ready and pickup-status feeds;
- kitchen, production, preparation, and batch-status signals;
- transaction, item-mix, modifier, and revenue ingestion;
- reconciliation and source-conflict handling.

Manual menu, price, availability, queue, and pickup communication remains core. Integrations must not block manual correction when data is delayed, wrong, or unavailable. Connector count, locations, transactions, refresh frequency, and history may be limits.

## AI-assisted capabilities

Optional AI candidates include:

- draft menu descriptions, promotions, service notices, and translations;
- layout, hierarchy, contrast, readability, and content-density suggestions;
- sell-out or demand-risk suggestions based on authorized data;
- route, event, and promotion planning assistance;
- image preparation and content adaptation;
- anomaly summaries for screen health, delivery, sales, or availability;
- natural-language analytics and operational summaries.

AI output must remain reviewable, attributable, reversible, and bounded by permissions. AI may suggest but must not independently publish, change prices, mark availability, infer legal/safety status, or overwrite authoritative source data without explicit approved workflow. AI usage may be a tier entitlement with metered limits or a separate add-on.

## Analytics and optimization

Optional analytics candidates include:

- location, event, route, service-window, stand, unit, and screen comparisons;
- promotion and menu-item performance;
- sell-out timing and availability-change analysis;
- queue, pickup, order, sales, and footfall analysis where data exists;
- content delivery, outdated-screen, retry, and recovery trends;
- multi-unit benchmarks and exception detection;
- forecast and recommendation models;
- scheduled reports, exports, and retained history.

Core operational visibility remains distinct from advanced analytics. Advanced analytics is a tier candidate; external sales, order, queue, traffic, weather, and footfall data are add-on candidates. Retention, refresh, export, and row-volume allowances are limits.

## Managed hardware, connectivity, and support

Optional service or add-on candidates include:

- managed screens and players;
- outdoor-rated equipment packages;
- cellular connectivity and managed routers;
- remote diagnostics and proactive monitoring;
- replacement, warranty, installation, and depot services;
- event deployment kits and temporary-screen rentals;
- priority support and guided setup;
- managed content or campaign services.

Basic pairing, publish confirmation, offline awareness, and restoration remain core regardless of hardware plan. Device count, data usage, support hours, replacement terms, and service regions are limits or commercial terms.

## External data and automation sources

Independent add-on candidates include:

- weather, traffic, map, routing, permit, event, venue, sponsor, and public-calendar data;
- queue, footfall, sensor, occupancy, and wait-time data;
- loyalty, CRM, email, messaging, notification, and social platforms;
- workforce, staffing, scheduling, and operations systems;
- delivery marketplace and catering platforms;
- digital menu networks and advertising exchanges.

All external data must preserve source identity, freshness, confidence, authority, failure state, and manual fallback. Unknown data remains unknown.

## Experience requirements

Optional capability surfaces must show:

- why the capability is useful and what remains available without it;
- whether access is included, purchasable, limited, unavailable, or administratively restricted;
- source, freshness, authority, and failure state for integrations;
- setup, empty, loading, permission, partial-success, disconnected, stale, conflict, and recovery states;
- clear scope before bulk or automated actions;
- preview and confirmation before high-impact publication;
- safe disable, disconnect, downgrade, and data-export behavior;
- accessibility, mobile use, outdoor readability, 200% zoom, keyboard access, and non-color-only status.

The project-local Impeccable `shape`, `harden`, and `polish` guidance informs this planning. No UI implementation is authorized.

## Owner decisions preserved

Later packaging work must decide:

- final tier names and bundles;
- which optional outcomes are included at each tier;
- which integrations remain independently purchasable;
- whether AI is bundled, metered, or an add-on;
- managed hardware and support commercial models;
- quantity, retention, refresh, transaction, and notification limits;
- downgrade, disconnect, export, and data-retention treatment;
- whether any partner or host integration requires contractual or regional availability.

## Validation

The optional catalog covers route and event scheduling, location publishing, advanced promotions, multi-unit coordination, venue and event integrations, POS and order systems, AI, analytics, managed hardware, and external data. It preserves essential manual operation as core and separates tier candidates, independent add-ons, limits, product state, permissions, and rollout controls.
