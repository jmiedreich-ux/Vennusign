## Restaurant inheritance

Restaurant remains the canonical baseline. Entertainment inherits general organization, venue, content, screen, targeting, publishing, delivery, version, user, role, permission, schedule, notice, localization, accessibility, analytics, and integration concepts.

The profile adds only meaningful Entertainment deltas: venues and experience hierarchies; attractions, exhibits, programs, events, sessions, queues, routes, gates and sections; timed and continuous occurrences; doors, starts, endings, last entry, boarding, seating and check-in; availability, delay, closure, cancellation, relocation, restriction, weather and reopening states; queue, wait, capacity and admission guidance; visitor wayfinding; and ticketing, admissions, access, venue, event, sports, map, rights-holder and managed-service boundaries.

No inherited general capability is redefined as an industry entitlement.

## Industry, subtype, and terminology

The industry boundary covers visitor-facing entertainment, cultural, attraction, screening, performance, event, sports, tour, activity, and mixed-destination operations where schedules, availability, disruptions, queues, capacity, admission, wayfinding, and screen communication are material.

The subtype model is bounded, has a neutral fallback, and resolves hybrids with one primary subtype plus descriptive traits. Subtype changes terminology, starter suggestions, screen-purpose recommendations, operating prompts, dashboard emphasis, and analytics presentation only. It does not grant permissions, tiers, add-ons, limits, privacy rights, or rollout access.

The terminology model consistently distinguishes venue, area, program, experience, event, session, occurrence, schedule, admission, ticket, pass, membership, reservation, timed entry, credential, queue, wait, capacity, notice, route, screen, publish, delivery, and restore. Public wording remains distinct from operator-only source, freshness, authority, target, and recovery detail.

## Operating model

The combined operating model covers venue operating days, local time zones, timed occurrences, continuous experiences, doors, last entry, boarding, seating, check-in, closures, delays, pauses, cancellations, relocations, restrictions, weather effects, reopening, event surges, queue/wait/capacity/admission guidance, wayfinding, accessibility, languages, sources, overrides, offline screens, partial delivery, outages, and restoration.

Track 0 does not invent legal, life-safety, accessibility-compliance, rights, licensing, gambling, alcohol, child-safety, biometric, camera, or jurisdiction-specific policy.

## Essential core

Every Entertainment & Attractions customer retains these core capabilities without a premium tier or paid integration:

1. Maintain basic venue, area, experience, schedule, occurrence, route, and visitor-information context.
2. Manually communicate available, limited, full, sold out, delayed, paused, closed, canceled, relocated, restricted, weather-affected, reopening, and unknown states with explicit scope.
3. Manually provide queue, wait, capacity, admission, boarding, seating, check-in, last-entry, and next-action guidance.
4. Maintain manual destinations, wayfinding, alternate routes, and customer-verified accessible-route wording.
5. Create manually authored notices, language variants, and accessibility-ready content.
6. Pair or select screens, choose purpose and context, and deliberately defer incomplete setup with a saved next action.
7. Select exact targets, preview public wording, language, effective time, source/freshness, and replaced content.
8. Publish and see accepted, pending, delivered, partial, failed, offline, outdated, incompatible, excluded, and unknown state by target.
9. Correct, supersede, unpublish, retry, undo where safe, and restore a prior successful version.
10. Continue manual operation when any external source is absent, disconnected, stale, conflicting, or unsupported.
11. Understand whether an action is blocked by permission, tier, add-on, limit, privacy, source, configuration, compatibility, or rollout.
12. Receive first-use, empty, validation, permission, stale, conflict, offline, partial, failure, success, and recovery guidance.

No ordinary essential operation depends on ticketing, admissions, access control, queue measurement, sensors, mapping, venue management, show control, collection management, attraction management, event or sports systems, CRM, POS, AI, advanced analytics, or managed hardware/service.

## Classification

Each concern has one primary classification:

- **Core capability:** essential manual venue, experience, schedule, state, notice, route, target, publication, delivery, correction, and recovery workflow.
- **Product/domain state:** represented industry, subtype, hierarchy, experience, event, session, schedule, admission, queue, wait, capacity, route, notice, source, target, publication, screen, and delivery value.
- **Permission:** who may view, edit, approve, publish, restore, configure, export, administer, or purchase.
- **Tier candidate:** advanced native coordination, workflow, campaigns, localization, maps, analytics, portfolio governance, enterprise administration, and extended history.
- **Independent add-on:** external ticketing, admissions, access, queue, occupancy, footfall, maps, venue/cinema/show-control/collection/attraction/event/sports, CRM, POS, translation, AI, identity, hardware, connectivity, monitoring, support, and professional services.
- **Limit:** counts, volume, retention, refresh, storage, export, screens, venues, users, objects, sources, messages, calls, and consumption.
- **Rollout flag:** temporary release, migration, compatibility, provider-pilot, experiment, or emergency control.

These conditions must never be collapsed into a generic “unavailable” feature flag.
