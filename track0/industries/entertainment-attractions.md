# Entertainment & Attractions Industry Profile

## Identity

- **Industry:** Entertainment & Attractions
- **RWP range:** RWP-00.63 through RWP-00.74
- **Current status:** Venue-subtype model complete in this proposed merge state
- **Baseline:** Restaurant
- **Completed through:** RWP-00.64
- **Next sequential RWP:** RWP-00.65 — Business Terminology

## Purpose

This profile covers destination-, program-, exhibition-, performance-, recreation-, and attraction-led venues whose visitor experience depends on accurate schedules, admissions guidance, wayfinding, availability, queue, venue-state, safety, accessibility, and event information across changing operating periods and physical areas.

It inherits the Restaurant baseline. This document records only meaningful differences needed to guide terminology, operations, capability classification, packaging, onboarding, dashboards, analytics, and validation.

## Primary customer outcomes

In addition to the Restaurant baseline outcomes, operators must be able to:

- help visitors understand what is happening, where it is happening, when it starts, who it is for, and what action to take next;
- keep programs, performances, screenings, exhibits, activities, attractions, sessions, rounds, tours, admissions, queues, closures, delays, relocations, and accessibility guidance current;
- publish reliably to selected entrance, lobby, box-office, concourse, auditorium, gallery, exhibit, queue, ride, activity, food-and-beverage, retail, parking, transportation, staff, and outdoor displays;
- coordinate organization-wide brand and operational information while allowing venue-, building-, area-, attraction-, event-, session-, and screen-specific differences;
- communicate sold-out, limited, full, delayed, paused, canceled, relocated, weather-affected, unavailable, maintenance, safety, and recovery states without rebuilding content;
- support calm, accessible, multilingual, distance-readable presentation for visitors who may be unfamiliar with the venue, moving through crowds, or under time pressure;
- confirm that time-sensitive information reached every intended display and recover safely from stale, offline, or failed delivery.

## Inherited unchanged from Restaurant

Unless a later Entertainment & Attractions RWP records a meaningful exception, this industry inherits:

- reusable content creation, editing, duplication, archive, restore, preview, and publishing patterns;
- screen pairing and management, explicit targeting, delivery confirmation, offline and outdated detection, and prior-version recovery;
- basic layouts, themes, business hours, venue information, understandable errors, and recovery guidance;
- permissions, product-state separation, limit separation, and packaging discipline;
- candidate scheduling, dayparts, campaigns, coordinated screens, multi-venue sharing, brand controls, approvals, history, analytics, identity, AI, hardware, and integration capabilities.

Restaurant menu, category, item, price, dietary, availability, and special semantics remain inherited for concessions, bars, cafés, restaurants, and retail-like food outlets that use them. They are not the primary content model for an entertainment venue or attraction as a whole.

## Meaningful differences from Restaurant

### Program- and destination-led visitor journey

Entertainment information supports discovery, arrival, admission, orientation, waiting, participation, viewing, intermission, movement, purchase, disruption, and departure. Content priorities may change by event, performance, screening, attraction, exhibit, session, timed entry, crowd condition, weather, or venue state.

### Time-bound and continuously available experiences

A venue may run overlapping performances, films, tours, talks, games, activities, exhibits, sessions, rounds, or timed-entry windows while also operating continuously available, self-guided, rotating, seasonal, or capacity-controlled experiences. Venue opening state must remain distinct from the state of a specific event, attraction, exhibit, session, activity, queue, or area.

### Physical hierarchy and visitor movement

An operation may contain campuses, buildings, entrances, gates, floors, zones, galleries, auditoriums, screens, stages, fields, courts, lanes, rides, exhibits, queues, food outlets, retail, parking, transportation points, and temporary event spaces. These are product/domain contexts, not entitlements. Manual wayfinding remains core even without mapping or ticketing integrations.

### Capacity, admission, queue, and availability states

Visitors may need to know whether an event is on sale, sold out, full, delayed, boarding, seating, paused, closed, weather-affected, relocated, canceled, or available through another session. Manual communication of these states is essential daily operation and cannot depend on a premium integration.

### Public, member, participant, and staff audiences

Displays may serve ticket buyers, members, school groups, families, tourists, participants, spectators, performers, vendors, sponsors, and staff. Audience, admission status, privacy, authorization, and content authority remain separate from commercial access. Public signage must not expose visitor-specific, ticket-specific, membership, participant, performer, security, or sensitive operational information by default.

### High-motion, crowded, outdoor, and low-light environments

Visitors may be walking, queuing, seated at a distance, outdoors, in dim auditoriums or galleries, in bright concourses, or surrounded by competing visual and audio stimuli. Essential information requires strong hierarchy, restrained motion, non-color status cues, concise language, and dependable portrait, landscape, large-format, mobile, and distance-reading behavior.

## Content and screen-purpose differences

An Entertainment & Attractions venue may use:

- entrance, welcome, admissions, membership, ticketing, box-office, and security guidance;
- today-now-next program boards, showtimes, screenings, performances, events, sessions, tours, talks, classes, games, and activity schedules;
- auditorium, screen, stage, gallery, exhibit, attraction, ride, lane, court, field, room, gate, and zone assignments;
- queue, wait-time, boarding, seating, capacity, sold-out, delay, pause, cancellation, relocation, reopening, and weather notices;
- campus, building, floor, concourse, parking, transportation, exit, accessibility, restroom, food, retail, and service wayfinding;
- exhibit, collection, interpretation, education, conservation, sponsor, donor, artist, performer, team, film, production, and attraction information;
- intermission, interval, closing-time, last-entry, last-ride, final-session, and departure guidance;
- venue-wide operational, safety, emergency, evacuation, shelter, and recovery communication;
- staff-facing operational information where permissions and privacy remain explicit;
- brand, atmosphere, trailer, highlight, promotion, merchandise, food-and-beverage, donor, sponsor, and destination content where essential guidance remains dominant.

## Industry boundary

### Included as native concepts

- cinemas and motion-picture exhibition venues;
- performing-arts theaters and live-performance venues;
- museums, non-retail galleries, science centers, planetariums, halls of fame, and interpretive institutions;
- zoos, aquariums, botanical gardens, wildlife parks, and similar visitor attractions;
- theme parks, amusement parks, water parks, and mixed-attraction parks;
- family entertainment centers, arcades, bowling centers, and activity-led recreation venues;
- spectator sports venues, arenas, stadiums, fields, courts, and event-led sports facilities;
- attractions, tours, visitor centers, heritage sites, and destination experiences with a managed on-site visitor journey;
- related hybrids where entertainment, exhibition, performance, recreation, or attraction attendance is the primary operating identity.

### Included through mixed-industry behavior

An Entertainment & Attractions organization may contain Restaurant, Bar/Brewery/Nightlife, Café/Bakery/Dessert, Food Truck/Concession, Hospitality, retail, parking, transportation, membership, education, and other operations. Those local venues may use their own approved business type while sharing organization-level brand, users, libraries, analytics, and commercial authority.

### Outside the canonical boundary

The native profile excludes, unless a managed visitor-facing entertainment or attraction operation is also present:

- content production, distribution, broadcasting, streaming, recording, publishing, or artist-management businesses without an on-site visitor venue;
- retail art dealers or commercial galleries primarily selling objects;
- private clubs, gyms, fitness studios, community recreation programs, and participant sports operations without a managed spectator or attraction-led visitor experience;
- gambling-only operations, lotteries, online gaming, and gaming-machine suppliers;
- restaurants, bars, nightlife venues, hotels, retail centers, offices, schools, houses of worship, and transportation facilities where entertainment is incidental;
- public parks, trails, beaches, natural areas, and informal outdoor sites without a managed attraction, program, admissions, or visitor-information operation;
- temporary private events without a durable venue or operator-managed visitor-information use case.

These boundaries determine Vennusign defaults and profile selection only. They are not legal, licensing, accessibility, safety, capacity, film-rating, gaming, labor, tax, statistical, or admission classifications.

## Canonical venue-subtype model

Subtype is selected from the dominant daily visitor journey and information rhythm. It does not classify legal status, venue size, brand, ownership, management, promoter, presenter, licensing, statistical category, or commercial package.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cinema** | Motion-picture screenings and auditorium showtimes control the visitor journey | Film, screening, auditorium/screen, format, rating/accessibility, start time, seating/boarding, sold-out, delay, intermission where used, and next screening | Drive-ins remain Cinema with outdoor and vehicle-based traits; a planetarium is Museum unless showtime-led operation truly dominates |
| **Performing Arts Theater** | A purpose-built house presents plays, dance, opera, orchestral, resident-company, or similar live performance | Production, performance, stage/auditorium, curtain/start time, seating, interval, cast/creative information, accessibility, late-seating, and performance state | Flexible promoter-led concert/comedy facilities are Live-Event Venue |
| **Museum** | Collection stewardship, preservation, interpretation, education, or institutional exhibits control the experience | Collection, exhibit, gallery/zone, timed entry, tours, programs, donor/sponsor context, access information, closures, and interpretation | Exhibition-led spaces without institutional collection stewardship may use Gallery / Exhibition Venue |
| **Gallery / Exhibition Venue** | Rotating exhibitions, installations, art presentation, trade or public exhibitions, or non-retail display programs dominate | Exhibition, installation, hall/gallery, artist/exhibitor, opening, schedule, wayfinding, capacity, talks, and temporary closures | Retail art dealers remain outside; permanent institutional collection stewardship points to Museum |
| **Zoo / Aquarium** | Living collections, habitats, conservation, and a managed visitor path dominate | Species/habitat, feeding/talk schedules, exhibit availability, route, accessibility, weather, animal-care closures, conservation, and last-entry guidance | Botanical gardens fit here when living collection and visitor path dominate |
| **Theme / Amusement Park** | Multiple rides, attractions, lands, shows, queues, and destination-wide operating states control the day | Attraction status, wait/queue, height/access guidance, showtimes, lands/zones, weather, maintenance, closures, reopening, last ride, and multi-area wayfinding | Water parks use a water-based trait; a single managed walkthrough or guided experience may be Attraction / Tour |
| **Family Entertainment Center** | A balanced mix of activities, party/group use, and repeatable participation controls the operation | Activity availability, session/party timing, lanes/areas, age/height/access guidance, queue, capacity, packages where represented, and multi-activity wayfinding | A specialist arcade or bowling-led concept uses its own subtype |
| **Arcade** | Coin, card, token, redemption, simulator, or game-floor operation is primary | Game zones, card/token guidance, tournaments, availability, age/access guidance, prizes where represented, events, and floor wayfinding | A balanced multi-activity destination is Family Entertainment Center |
| **Bowling Center** | Lanes, games/rounds, leagues, tournaments, and bowling scheduling dominate | Lane assignment, availability, league/tournament schedules, check-in, session state, shoe/service guidance, scoring notices, and food/arcade secondary content | Incidental arcade or food does not change subtype; balanced multi-activity operation may use Family Entertainment Center |
| **Sports Venue** | Spectator competition, a home-team calendar, tournament, match, race, or sporting event controls operations | Event/match, team/competitor, gate, section, field/court/track, start time, score/status where sourced, concessions, parking, transport, safety, and egress | Multi-use arenas use a multi-use trait; promoter-led changing productions may make Live-Event Venue more accurate |
| **Live-Event Venue** | Promoter-led concerts, comedy, festivals, conferences, conventions, or changing productions control the calendar | Event, artist/speaker/production, doors, start time, stage/room, gate, seating/standing, schedule, delay/cancel/relocate, merchandise, sponsor, and egress | Purpose-built resident performing-arts houses use Performing Arts Theater |
| **Attraction / Tour** | A managed route, guided destination, observation experience, heritage visit, immersive walkthrough, or single primary attraction controls the journey | Tour/experience, departure or entry window, route, guide, language, accessibility, weather, capacity, last entry, safety, closure, and reopening | Broader multi-activity destinations may be Family Entertainment Center or Theme / Amusement Park |

### Neutral fallback

A venue may remain **Unspecified / General Entertainment & Attraction Venue** when no supported subtype clearly controls the daily visitor journey and information rhythm. The fallback is product/domain state, not a reduced plan or hidden commercial package.

### Hybrid traits

Use one primary subtype plus optional descriptive traits when secondary characteristics matter. Approved trait families include:

- outdoor, drive-in or vehicle-based, water-based, seasonal, and weather-sensitive;
- immersive, heritage, living-collection, science or education, and interpretation-led;
- general-admission, reserved-seat, timed-entry, membership-led, participant-led, and spectator-led;
- multi-use, campus, touring, resident-company, team-home, tournament, and festival;
- low-light, high-motion, continuous, self-guided, guided, and temporary.

Traits tune future terminology candidates, starter recommendations, screen-purpose suggestions, operating guidance, and presentation emphasis. They do not stack entitlements, grant capabilities, transfer authority, or increase limits.

### Resolved ambiguous concepts

- **Drive-in:** Cinema with outdoor and vehicle-based traits.
- **Water park:** Theme / Amusement Park with a water-based trait.
- **Botanical garden:** Zoo / Aquarium when living collection and visitor path dominate.
- **Planetarium or observatory:** Museum when education, interpretation, and exhibits dominate; Cinema or Performing Arts Theater only when showtime-led operation truly controls the venue.
- **Historical site:** Museum when preservation and interpretation dominate; Attraction / Tour when a managed route or guided destination experience dominates.
- **Escape room, haunted attraction, observation deck, cave, scenic route, or single immersive walkthrough:** Attraction / Tour unless a broader multi-activity destination makes Family Entertainment Center more accurate.
- **Trampoline park or mixed miniature-golf, laser-tag, climbing, or party destination:** Family Entertainment Center when no single specialist activity controls the operation.
- **Bowling with incidental arcade or food:** Bowling Center; a balanced multi-activity destination may use Family Entertainment Center.
- **Arena or stadium:** Sports Venue when competition or the home-team calendar dominates; Live-Event Venue when promoter-led productions dominate. A multi-use trait records secondary behavior.
- **Purpose-built live-performance house:** Performing Arts Theater; a flexible concert, comedy, festival, or event facility is Live-Event Venue.
- **Exhibition-led non-retail art space:** Gallery / Exhibition Venue; collection stewardship and institutional interpretation make Museum more accurate.
- **Resort or casino with lodging:** Hospitality at the property level, with local Entertainment venues using the appropriate subtype.

## Organization, venue, and subtype behavior

- Organization primary industry may seed a subtype suggestion but cannot override a venue-local selection.
- Different venues in one organization may select different subtypes.
- A campus, park, arena district, cultural complex, resort, casino, or destination may contain multiple locally typed venues.
- Restaurants, bars, cafés, concessions, lodging, retail, and other embedded operations may use their own approved business types.
- Shared organization brand, users, libraries, analytics, and commercial authority remain organization concerns.
- Venue-, area-, attraction-, exhibit-, event-, session-, queue-, and screen-specific terminology, content, operating state, target selection, and defaults remain local.
- Ownership, operator, promoter, presenter, tenant, sponsor, team, performer, distributor, rights-holder, or host relationships do not silently transfer permissions, commercial access, content authority, or visitor data.
- Copying content between subtypes preserves the authored content and its authority scope; subtype-specific recommendations may be offered without silently rewriting it.

## Capability-classification decisions through RWP-00.64

1. Organization primary industry, primary Entertainment subtype, neutral subtype state, and optional descriptive traits are **product/domain state**.
2. Venue, campus, building, entrance, floor, zone, destination, auditorium, screen, stage, gallery, exhibit, attraction, ride, lane, court, field, room, event, performance, screening, session, queue, admission window, capacity state, delay, closure, relocation, and similar values remain **product/domain state** independent of subtype.
3. Manual program, showtime, event, attraction, exhibit, admissions, wayfinding, queue, capacity, delay, closure, relocation, accessibility, safety, targeting, publishing, delivery confirmation, offline awareness, and recovery remain **core capabilities**.
4. Authorization, audience, admission, privacy, and content authority are **permission or scope concerns**, not commercial access.
5. Counts of venues, campuses, buildings, areas, attractions, exhibits, events, performances, screenings, sessions, queues, screens, users, integrations, storage, retained history, or AI consumption are **usage or quantity limits**.
6. Automatic ticketing, admissions, access-control, queue-management, venue-management, cinema, show-control, collection-management, attraction, event, sports, or related synchronization remains a future **independent add-on candidate** or later approved tier bundle and cannot replace manual core operation.
7. Visitor-specific, ticket-specific, seat-specific, member-specific, participant-specific, performer-specific, sponsor-specific, security-sensitive, or operationally sensitive information requires later privacy and authorization decisions and is not assumed to be public signage content.

## Impeccable planning guardrails

The project-local Impeccable skill and `shape` guidance apply to future subtype selection and change flows.

- The administrative surface is **Operate** mode for an owner, administrator, or authorized venue manager.
- “Best when” definitions must use the dominant visitor journey, program rhythm, physical movement, admissions model, schedule pattern, collection or attraction structure, and operational state rather than legal or marketing classifications.
- One primary subtype, a neutral fallback, and optional descriptive traits must be understandable without implying plan differences.
- A change flow must preview changed defaults, explain preservation, require confirmation, support safe cancellation, and cover permission-restricted, validation-failure, interrupted-save, success, and restoration states.
- Phone and desktop layouts must remain scannable; overlap detail should use progressive disclosure; keyboard, assistive technology, localization expansion, and 200% zoom must be supported; color cannot be the only distinction.
- Planning must scale from a single-screen cinema or small gallery to multi-building museums, zoological campuses, amusement parks, arena districts, sports complexes, and mixed entertainment destinations.
- Preserve the approved Sky Blue direction for administrative surfaces. Visitor-facing identity may vary by venue without weakening operational hierarchy.

No UI or product implementation is authorized by these planning decisions.
