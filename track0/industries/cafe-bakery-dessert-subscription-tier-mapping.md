# Café, Bakery & Dessert Subscription Tier Mapping

## Authority and scope

This document proposes customer-outcome subscription groupings for Café, Bakery & Dessert. The names are planning labels only. No pricing, billing, entitlement records, live gates, checkout behavior, or implementation is approved.

The proposal inherits the Restaurant and Bar progression: safe daily operation is universally included; advanced planning and coordination may be tiered; portfolio governance may be tiered separately; external systems, managed services, and metered services remain independent add-on candidates; quantities remain limits.

## Packaging principles

1. Essential manual daily operation remains core at every subscription level.
2. Industry and subtype are non-commercial product configuration.
3. Permissions, product state, tier access, add-ons, limits, privacy/source rules, and rollout controls remain separate.
4. Native workflow may be tiered when value increases through automation, coordination, or scale.
5. External, physical, managed, custom, or metered services remain independent add-on candidates where value or cost is separable.
6. Upgrade and downgrade preserve customer-authored content, current safe publication, manual fallback, source/freshness context, delivery confidence, and recovery.
7. Final names, prices, allowances, and feature boundaries require cross-industry owner approval.

## Proposed outcome architecture

### Operate — universally included core

Outcome: keep today’s products, hours, service context, pickup information, screens, and public messages accurate and safely visible.

Included capabilities:

- venue information, regular and temporary hours, service periods, closures, reopening, and public instructions;
- menu, product, category, price, description, image, size, option, and customer-authored label management;
- rapid available, unavailable, sold-out, limited, next-batch, available-again, preorder, pickup, batch, and freshness-guidance updates;
- public preorder, custom-order, and pickup presentation without private fulfillment data;
- screen pairing, purpose, explicit targeting, preview, and immediate publication;
- per-target delivery confirmation, offline/outdated awareness, correction, retry, supersession, undo, and restoration;
- ordinary permissions and clear separation of access, authority, source, state, and limits; and
- core operational visibility for current content, sources, screens, publication, delivery, and recovery.

Operate is a complete useful product outcome, not a teaser. Urgent correction, delivery confidence, and recovery may not be paywalled.

### Coordinate — plan, promote, and streamline

Outcome: reduce repeated setup and coordinate recurring customer communication.

Candidate capabilities:

- recurring service periods, product rotations, batch messages, specials, seasonal collections, and exception calendars;
- reusable daypart, menu, promotion, collection, and pickup templates;
- planned availability and return transitions with conflict review;
- campaign calendars, reusable campaigns, coordinated start/stop/expiration, and richer screen-purpose variants;
- advanced presentation, brand libraries, reusable components, and localization workflow;
- configurable approvals, additional history, comparison, and coordinated multi-screen workflow; and
- advanced product, promotion, service-period, content-freshness, and operational-response analytics based on available Vennusign evidence.

Failure, expiry, limit, or downgrade conditions must not block immediate manual operation.

### Portfolio — scale and govern

Outcome: coordinate multiple venues, brands, teams, screens, and local variations safely.

Candidate capabilities:

- organization and regional libraries with controlled local inheritance and overrides;
- safe multi-venue copying, distribution, bulk action, mixed-state review, and partial-success recovery;
- organization-wide campaign, schedule, template, language, and screen governance;
- separation of author, reviewer, and publisher responsibilities;
- advanced audit, longer history, saved reports, comparative portfolio analysis, and advanced export;
- cross-venue source, freshness, delivery, and exception oversight; and
- enterprise administration and policy controls separate from ordinary operational permissions.

Portfolio behavior preserves each venue’s subtype, terminology, local time, products, state, sources, permissions, targets, and restore points.

### Enterprise — administration, risk, and service

Outcome: support complex organizations requiring enterprise administration, identity, governance, audit, support, and negotiated service behavior.

Candidate capabilities:

- enterprise identity and provisioning workflow;
- advanced audit, policy, risk, data-governance, and administration controls;
- complex portfolio structures and delegated administration;
- negotiated retention, export, support, service, monitoring, and operational-response workflow; and
- enterprise reporting and controlled integration administration.

Enterprise does not automatically include separately operated external systems, managed hardware, or metered services.

## Relationship to Restaurant and Bar

Café uses the same cross-industry progression rather than introducing venue-specific commercial packages:

1. **Operate:** accurate current information, screens, delivery, and recovery.
2. **Coordinate:** schedules, rotations, campaigns, richer presentation, approvals, and workflow.
3. **Portfolio:** shared libraries, local inheritance, bulk coordination, governance, and advanced analysis.
4. **Enterprise:** identity, administration, audit, risk, service, and complex-portfolio controls.

Café terminology and recommendations emphasize batches, sell-outs, seasonal products, preorder, pickup, flavor or option presentation, and freshness guidance. These differences affect presentation, not entitlement.

## Independent add-on candidates

The following remain independently selectable unless later owner review approves a bounded included subset:

- POS and catalog synchronization;
- inventory and production systems;
- ordering, payment, fulfillment, ready-state, and customer-notification systems;
- loyalty, CRM, messaging, identity, and attribution services;
- supplier, calendar, weather, event, and traffic sources;
- premium translation and language services;
- externally metered AI, content, image, analysis, or optimization services;
- managed players, screens, installation, connectivity, monitoring, replacement, and enhanced support;
- managed content, localization, analytics, or campaign services; and
- custom integrations, transformations, exports, and customer-specific data services.

Each add-on defines authorization, privacy, source authority, freshness, coverage, mapping, conflict, limits, disconnect, cancellation, retention, manual fallback, and recovery.

## Organization and venue inheritance

- Commercial tier access is normally evaluated at organization scope unless an approved policy explicitly supports venue-level purchases.
- Organization industry selects initial terminology and recommendations only.
- A venue may select any supported business type without changing the subscription by itself.
- A venue inherits organization-available capabilities but still requires valid local permission and object scope.
- Mixed-industry organizations receive one coherent capability architecture presented with venue-appropriate terminology.
- Subtypes and descriptive traits do not stack or unlock capabilities.
- Organization-wide actions preview venue type, local time, current state, source, permission, targets, and impact before publication.

## Limits remain separate

Candidate limits include venues, screens, users, roles, approvers, products, categories, menus, options, layouts, templates, languages, schedules, campaigns, versions, history, storage, exports, reports, integrations, connected accounts, sync frequency, transactions, messages, translations, monitoring endpoints, support incidents, data, and AI consumption.

A reached limit identifies the exact dimension and scope, shows current use and allowance, preserves existing data and published content, permits safe reduction or plan review, and remains distinct from permission denial, tier access, add-on ownership, setup, disconnection, stale source, unsupported context, rollout, or business state.

Essential correction and recovery remain possible when an optional limit is reached.

## Upgrade behavior

Upgrade should:

- preserve current content, products, state, sources, screens, targets, and settings;
- unlock outcomes without changing permission assignments;
- explain newly available workflow rather than implementation keys;
- identify configuration still required;
- avoid automatic publication, inheritance, source, or organization-wide changes; and
- allow preview and deliberate adoption of schedules, templates, campaigns, policies, and portfolio behavior.

Onboarding must reach first-screen value without requiring an upgrade. Pricing remains accessible but should normally appear contextually after the operator understands the included path.

## Downgrade behavior

Downgrade should:

- preserve customer-authored data, current safe published versions, basic recovery history, and manual core operation;
- prevent new advanced-object creation without silently deleting existing objects;
- identify affected schedules, campaigns, approvals, templates, reports, inheritance, and synchronized displays before the effective date;
- provide conversion, detachment, export, archive, grace-period, or bounded read-only options where safe;
- define whether active schedules finish, pause, or require manual conversion;
- retain source/freshness and delivery context needed for safe operation;
- separate tier loss from permission loss, add-on failure, and limit overage; and
- avoid breaking current screen delivery or hiding offline/outdated state.

Add-on cancellation separately defines disconnect, source fallback, data retention, device/service return, support termination, and consumption reconciliation.

## Packaging risks

- Gating rapid availability, publication confidence, or recovery would make the product unsafe and impractical.
- Subtype-specific tiers would confuse defaults with commercial access and fragment mixed organizations.
- Bundling variable-cost integrations or managed services into ordinary tiers may obscure cost and support responsibility.
- A single undifferentiated advanced tier may combine unrelated value.
- A large à-la-carte catalog of micro-features would be difficult to understand and administer.
- Venue or screen count alone does not represent operational complexity.
- Pricing prompts before first-screen activation may interrupt the aha moment.

## Owner decisions required later

- Final number, names, and prices of tiers.
- Whether Coordinate, Portfolio, and Enterprise remain separate commercial levels.
- Exact boundaries among scheduling, campaigns, approvals, localization, analytics, and portfolio governance.
- Which analytics remain included, tiered, externally sourced, or premium add-ons.
- Whether any bounded AI or translation allowance is included.
- Exact limit values, overage, grace, archive, read-only, and downgrade behavior.
- Trial access and post-trial behavior.
- Direct, partner, or marketplace delivery of integrations and managed services.
- Whether any venue-level independent add-on purchase is supported.

## Impeccable planning implications

Plan comparison and locked-capability surfaces use customer outcomes, keep core actions visible, preserve work, and distinguish upgrade required, add-on required, permission denied, setup incomplete, limit reached, disconnected, stale, unsupported, rollout-disabled, and business-state conditions. They support keyboard use, 200% zoom, localization expansion, non-color status, restrained motion, and mobile/desktop adaptation.
