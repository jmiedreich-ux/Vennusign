Quiet signals for a stale board

Six ways to tell staff the screen has stopped listening, without telling a room full of guests that something is broken.

What ships today puts a black box in the top-left corner of the wall that reads “Live updates unavailable — current content remains on screen.” It appears in three of the four connection states — connecting, reconnecting and degraded — so a guest sees it during an ordinary reconnect as well as a real outage.

The message is accurate and it is aimed at the wrong person. A guest reading a menu cannot act on it, cannot fix it, and now knows only that the bar’s systems are unwell. Meanwhile the thing they actually care about — whether these prices are real — is unaddressed.

The distinction the product already draws is audience, not honesty. Both stay honest; they use different words.

01

Say nothing at all

A brief reconnect changes nothing a guest can perceive: the prices on the wall are still the prices. The board stays silent and the truth goes where someone can act on it — the Screens page, which already reports Online and Offline per screen.

This is the whole fix for connecting and reconnecting. Those states are the network doing its job, not a fault.

The mark beside the venue name

The dot before My Bar is part of the board’s furniture in every state. When the screen stops listening it desaturates and breathes slowly. Staff who have been told what it means read it across the room; a guest reads a design detail, because that is all it is.

It carries no words, so it cannot be misread, and it costs no layout — the mark is already there.

03

A time, not a status

“Menu as of 7:42 pm” states a fact about the content instead of a fault in the plumbing. A guest who reads it learns something mildly useful and nothing alarming; a member of staff who knows the board normally says nothing learns the screen has been cut off since 7:42.

It only earns its place once the gap is long enough to matter — printed on a board that is thirty seconds behind, it invites a question nobody needed to ask.

04

Diagnostics on demand

Everything an engineer wants — how long, last content, revision, screen id — held behind a deliberate gesture: a long press in a corner, a key on the paired remote, or ?staff=1. It shows for fifteen seconds and leaves.

This is strictly better than a permanent banner even for the person it was written for, because a banner can only afford six words and this can afford everything.

05

Withhold the claims you can’t stand behind

A stale board’s real danger is not that it looks stale — it is that Sold out may have been lifted an hour ago, or a happy-hour price may have expired. Rather than announce a fault, stop making the claims that depend on being current, and let the durable ones stand.

The hairline along the bottom edge is the only visible trace: a colour a guest never decodes and staff learn in a shift.

06

Guest words, and only at the end

Past the point where the board may genuinely mislead — an hour, a shift — silence stops being kind. But the honest sentence is about what the guest should do, not about a socket that will not open.

It is hospitality copy, so it belongs to the venue: a line they can write themselves, the way they choose the fallback card in decision 14.

Adopt lastThe only guest-visible words, and the venue writes them.

Disclosure follows risk, not connection state

The current banner escalates on the wrong axis — it fires the moment a socket drops, and says the same thing at four seconds as at four hours. What actually changes over time is the chance the board is wrong, and that is what a guest has any stake in.

ElapsedWhat it meansGuest seesStaff sees
0 – 60s A reconnect. Content is still correct. Nothing. Nothing on the wall; Screens page still reads Online.
1 – 15 min A real drop. Content almost certainly still correct. Nothing. The venue mark dims and breathes. Screens page flips to Offline.
15 – 60 min An 86 or a price change could have been missed. Volatile claims withdrawn — no sold-out marks, no happy-hour pricing. Dimmed mark, bottom hairline, full detail on demand.
60 min + The board may be actively misleading. “Please ask us about today’s specials” — the venue’s own line. All of the above, plus the timestamp printed.
