One extensible content model

All industries share ten primitives: collection, section, entry, attributes, operating state, timing, place/audience, source, presentation and lifecycle. Industry modules add relevant fields—food and allergens; beverage formats; batch/freshness; events and admission; attraction queues/capacity; hospitality location and service conditions—without forcing every entry into a restaurant-item schema.

Recommended Content-area architecture

Content
├── Library       Find, create, duplicate, archive and restore
├── Editor        Structure, entries and industry properties
├── Quick Update  Change urgent public states without the full editor
├── Plan          Timing, service periods, schedules and expiry
├── Presentation  Layout, theme, media, language and accessibility
├── Sources       Authority, freshness, mappings, conflicts and overrides
└── Publish       Targets, change review, delivery evidence and recovery

The main editor should combine a structure outline, direct preview/canvas, contextual property inspector, persistent draft-versus-live status and exact publish impact. Quick Update stays separate for interrupted operational work.

Backlog — TBD

No phases and no commitment. The owner canceled former Phases 14–16 as numbered phases. Their ideas remain only as an unprioritized backlog and require fresh approval before planning or implementation.

Mobile operations — formerly Phase 14

Intent: fast operational control from iOS and Android during active service.

  • Shared React Native application, biometric authentication, and operational push notifications.
  • Quick unavailable/“86” updates, daily specials, happy-hour overrides, and emergency broadcasts.
  • Mobile fleet health, offline alerts, last-seen evidence, manual publish, and remote content refresh.
  • Brewery-specific keg-empty, tap-list, and “Now Pouring” actions.

Long horizon

AI-assisted work — formerly Phase 15

Intent: lower the skill required to produce, arrange, and troubleshoot content while keeping people in control.

  • Description drafting, bulk generation, naming suggestions, and human-confirmed allergen suggestions.
  • Plain-language custom display generation with reusable templates, governed variables, sandboxing, usage tracking, and review before publish.
  • A developer HTML/CSS editor with supported variables and live venue-data preview.
  • POS-informed recommendations, layout advice, and AI-assisted branded imagery.
  • Page-aware support and engineering diagnostic agents with consent, evidence, privacy, and access controls.

Intent: use accumulated operational and commerce evidence to demonstrate reliability and guide decisions.

Rules that shape every screen

  1. Design for an owner working in interrupted bursts: current scope, public impact, exceptions, and the next safe action come first.
  2. Preserve essential manual operation when automation, integrations, or paid add-ons fail.
  3. Preview exact targets and distinguish intended from delivered results.
  4. Never collapse permission, entitlement, product state, add-on state, source health, limits, restrictions, and rollout into one generic lock.
  5. Preview consequences for destructive or high-scope actions and provide recovery where possible.
  6. Cover loading, first use, empty, validation, success, error, denial, stale/offline, responsive, keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, and localization states.
  7. Industry changes language, defaults, and emphasis—not entitlement.

Owner decisions still required

  • Complete V1, V1.1, V2, and Later outcomes.
  • Final capability names, identifiers, tiers, add-ons, prices, trials, contracts, and regions.
  • Exact allowances, pooling, grace, overage, retention, and grandfathering.
  • Supported providers, hardware platforms, service levels, and responsibility boundaries.
  • Privacy, rights, safety, regulated-content, AI, and data-governance policy.
  • Final top-level navigation and which future areas stay hidden until useful.
