Executive conclusion

Vennu has substantial operational capability for internally provisioned venues: Platform Operations operations, Back Office content management, display pairing, billing foundations, realtime behavior, and POS integrations are all represented in the codebase.

Primary product gap A new customer cannot independently create an account, select a trial or paid tier, create a venue, obtain authorized access, activate a first screen, and return later through a normal identity/session model.

The system currently contains the necessary operational pieces, but they are disconnected. It behaves as an internally provisioned operations platform rather than a self-service customer product. Phase 13 is the correct foundation to close this gap.

Product-surface findings

Platform Operations

Strengths: dashboard, venue support, tier/feature management, operational controls, and support context.

Missing lifecycle support: customer identity, owner/membership context, onboarding queue, onboarding timeline, verification/invite/passkey recovery actions, and customer activation diagnostics.

Back Office

Strengths: menu, screens, themes, schedules, tap list, billing, capability-aware routes, and upgrade guidance.

Missing lifecycle support: real sign-in, first-run onboarding, account/team settings, recovery, provider management, and an Account ? Plan ? Venue ? First Screen ? Go Live timeline.

Display player

Strengths: pairing, provisioning, platform bootstrap, and normal display operation.

Missing lifecycle support: connection to customer onboarding, entitlement-aware activation, pairing recovery, and customer-visible device state.

Billing and tiers

Strengths: Stripe Checkout/Portal, webhook processing, public tiers, screen limits, and feature resolution.

Missing lifecycle support: tier-defined trials, pre-venue entitlement, checkout recovery, and customer acquisition entry points.

Security and operational preflight

Do this before exposing public customer acquisition. The identity foundation should not be released until secret handling, public endpoint intent, rate limiting, authorization, audit requirements, and abuse controls are explicitly decided.
  1. Move secret-like values from committed runtime configuration to secure deployment/user-secret configuration.
  2. Decide whether public venue creation and screen registration routes are intentional; protect, remove, or rate-limit them accordingly.
  3. Define organization-level tenant isolation before creating customer-facing routes.
  4. Use OAuth/OIDC state, nonce, PKCE, strict callback allowlists, and verified provider subject identifiers.
  5. Use passkeys as the preferred returning-user path; use TOTP for enrolled fallback/step-up; keep email links for recovery/fallback.
  6. Keep Stripe webhooks authoritative for paid entitlement; never trust a return URL as payment proof.
  7. Audit membership, billing, ownership, entitlement, and display-pairing changes.

Test coverage assessment

The current test inventory is strong for implemented operations: Platform Operations authorization, billing/webhooks, venue provisioning, screens/pairing, menus, themes, schedules, HaaS, POS providers, migrations, and Phase 12 critical journeys.

The following tests are absent because the corresponding product capabilities are absent:

Identity tests

  • Google/Apple callback and account linking
  • Email verification and collision handling
  • Passkey enrollment/assertion
  • TOTP enrollment, recovery, and step-up
  • Session revoke and cross-device behavior

Onboarding tests

  • No-card tier-defined trial lifecycle
  • Checkout pending and webhook-confirmed activation
  • Abandoned onboarding resume
  • First-screen entitlement and pairing
  • Platform Operations/customer timeline states

Recommended ongoing planning gate

Every new product phase should begin with a product gap register and a complete customer-journey review. Every UI package should then perform the documented UX MCP consultation and page/screen function gap analysis.
